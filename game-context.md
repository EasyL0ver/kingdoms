# Kingdoms — Game Context & Vision

## Overview

**Kingdoms** is a medieval-themed, cards-only tabletop game for **4–10 players**. Inspired by the political intrigue of Game of Thrones, it centers on cutthroat, freeform politics where alliances form and shatter organically. There is no board, no dice, no tokens — only cards.

## Core Philosophy

- **Cards only.** Every mechanic lives on a card. No external components.
- **Cards ARE the rules.** There is no external turn structure, no phase system, no implicit rules to memorise. If a rule exists, it's printed on a card. Drawing cards happens through other cards. Game zones (play areas, markets, thrones) are established by cards. A player should never need to consult a rulebook mid-game — everything they need is in their hand or on the table.
- **Zero mental load from rules.** Unlike games where you must learn an implicit framework (untap → upkeep → draw, etc.), Kingdoms front-loads all complexity onto card text. The "rules" document is essentially: *read your cards, do what they say, negotiate freely.* New players can sit down and play by reading what's in front of them.
- **Asymmetry through conflict.** Players don't start with unique factions. Asymmetry *emerges* from the game — one player may claim the title of King while others decide whether to follow or overthrow.
- **Politics first.** Negotiation, betrayal, alliances, and power plays are the heart of the game. Mechanics support but never replace the social layer.
- **Dynamic win condition.** The game doesn't have a single predetermined win condition. Whichever deck is depleted first determines *how* the winner is scored — making every game's endgame different.
- **Customisable experience.** The group selects which decks to include before each session, and future expansions can be mixed in to curate the experience for the table.

## Setting

A loosely medieval world of rising and falling kingdoms. The setting is flavour, not simulation — it exists to support the themes of power, ambition, faith, family, and civilisation.

## Player Experience

Players begin on relatively equal footing. Through card acquisition, political manoeuvring, and strategic deck manipulation, the table's power dynamics shift constantly. A player who claims Kingship paints a target on themselves. A player quietly accumulating Priest cards may suddenly find the Religion deck depleted — and themselves victorious.

The game rewards reading the room, making deals, knowing when to betray, and understanding which decks the table is racing toward.

## Design Goals

1. Every session should feel different based on group composition and deck selection.
2. No single dominant strategy — the shifting win condition prevents "solved" play.
3. Accessible entry point (Barbarian tier) with strategic depth (Empire tier).
4. Scalable from small intense games (4 players) to chaotic political arenas (10 players).
5. Expansion-friendly architecture — new decks slot into the tier system cleanly.
